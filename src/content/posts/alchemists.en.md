#  The Alchemical Echo of Modern Civilisation

This is a sharp and profound observation: the pioneers who studied chemistry were once mistaken for alchemists, and today, faced with frontier technology, the public still tends to see scientists as modern wizards wielding mysterious powers. That cognitive dislocation, stretched across time, is accelerating faster than ever in the age of AI.

As the science-fiction writer Arthur C. Clarke put it: "Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic." Once technology passes beyond human intuition and comprehension, it degrades from a science that reason can take apart into a magic that can only be revered.

---

# The Wall of Knowledge and the Laziness of Understanding

Why do we still live in the shadow of "high-tech alchemy"? Because modern science has built barriers of knowledge that are hard to climb:

## Scientific principles as a black box
Science in Newton's day was visual. Today's frontier technology (quantum physics, gene editing, neural networks) is **invisible** and **counter-intuitive**. To an ordinary person, a server driving global finance and a ritual circle summoning wind and rain work the same way: as an unfathomable **black box**. We only know that an input (a command, an incantation) yields an output (an effect, a spell); the chain of logic in between is entirely missing.

## Professional jargon as incantation
Ancient alchemists built barriers with obscure symbols and Latin. Modern science has its own "high language": complicated mathematical formulae and an ocean of technical terms. When terms like "CRISPR-Cas9" or "generative adversarial network" reach a general audience, they sound like **sacred incantations** only a few initiates can read, which cements the scientist's image as a **priest**.

## The cognitive principle of least effort
Truly understanding a complex scientific principle takes enormous cognitive effort. Attributing complicated phenomena to "black tech" or "magic" is a cognitive shortcut. This "cognitive miserliness" makes people prefer to stop asking and simply enjoy the results, treating scientists as unapproachable spellcasters.

---

# The Exponential Growth of the Magic Flame

In the AI era, this "alchemist" misunderstanding has been amplified exponentially, because AI has fundamentally changed what science is:

## The creator losing control: from architect to keeper
In the era of deep learning, scientists are no longer **architects of logic** in the way traditional programmers were; they are more like **keepers of data**. They pour enormous datasets into a vast "boiler" (a neural network), and after weeks of "distillation", the model **emerges** with abilities that even its developers cannot fully explain. This "**crisis of explainability**" leaves the creators themselves facing their own creation with humility; to the public it is nothing short of a miracle.

## Prompt engineering as the modern chanting of spells
This is the most ironic part. When users work with a large language model, they must enter a specific string of natural language or code: the so-called **prompt**. The practice is essentially identical to praying to a god or reciting an incantation to a spirit: a tiny difference in form, tone or keyword can change the result beyond recognition. The user is no longer operating a machine; they are holding a **seance**.

## Hallucination and the return of the divine
Traditional machines are cold and precise; AI is **probabilistic**. It will spout nonsense with complete confidence (hallucination) and it will produce startling insight. This "sometimes genius, sometimes deranged" quality makes it look far more like the capricious, unpredictable gods and spirits of ancient myth than a tool.

---

# When Science Becomes Faith: the Cost of the Alchemical Misreading

When science is alienated into magic and scientists are lifted onto an altar as alchemists, it poses serious risks to society:

## Pseudoscience proliferates
If science is magic, then the public's test of truth becomes "which one sounds more convincing". Real quantum physics is complex and hard; a fraud only needs to print the words "quantum" and "nano" on a product to harvest people's blind spots. The authority of science is diluted, and a breeding ground opens for every kind of commercial deception.

## The extreme pendulum of crowning and destroying
Magic is absolute. This leads the public to hold unrealistic expectations of scientists, and the moment scientific exploration makes an incremental correction or admits an error (which is precisely what scientific integrity looks like), it is immediately read as "fraud" or "failure". This violent swinging between blind faith and disappointment makes society's trust in experts extremely fragile.

## The dilemma of democracy and the silencing of ethics
Technologies such as AI and gene editing concern the fate of humanity and require broad public debate and ethical oversight. But if the public believes these are "the secret arts of alchemists", they will naturally assume they have no standing to take part. That can leave the right to interpret and decide technology monopolised by a small elite, until society sinks into **technological fatalism**: passively accepting the consequences of technology while being unable to shape its direction.

---

# The Ebbing of Reason

The greatest danger of the "alchemist" misunderstanding is that it marks **the ebbing of reason**. We live in an Eden built of "high-tech magic". We enjoy the blessings of technology but can no longer take the world apart, no longer believe that effort can lead to understanding the universe.

If this trend continues, human civilisation will divide: a tiny number of **priests** who hold the core principles keep the civilisation running, while the vast majority of **believers** enjoy the results and sink into blind faith and fear of the black box and the unknown.

Only by pulling science down from the altar and re-emphasising the value of critical thinking and rational inquiry can we keep our civilisation from becoming a **high-tech, low-understanding "new Middle Ages"**.
