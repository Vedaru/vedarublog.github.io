# Before we start  

An observer in some distant galaxy would probably not understand why we on this blue planet cry at the completion of one orbit.  
On a cosmic scale it is a negligible displacement; but for us, with our brief lives, that circle means farewell and rebirth.  
I often find the celebration absurd, and yet I inevitably fall into it.  
What is recorded here is the story of that one orbit: of a wasteland, of daybreak, of a frog.  
Reader, if you are not in a hurry, let me tell it.  

---

# The wasteland, and fading footprints 

June.
There was no cinematic scene, no tearful farewell, no sudden love letter.  
The end of high school was simply that the cicadas were a bit louder and the exam papers were finally finished.  

In that strictly partitioned stretch of time I was not an interesting person.  
To avoid unnecessary expenditure I folded myself away and practised an economy of energy. I did not talk, did not socialise, was like a transparent shadow.  
In that compressed silence I tried to think about systems, about freedom, about the meaning of being alive.  
Unfortunately, until the summer ended, I never reached an answer that convinced even myself.  

Then university.  
It was not the tranquil life I had imagined.  
I was like a goose on a lake, apparently gliding serenely, thrashing desperately beneath the surface.  
I read more books and took harder courses, and never felt as impoverished as I did then.  
Sometimes I notice with alarm that the soft part inside me is hardening.  
I am slowly becoming a boring, faceless adult, the kind I used to dislike.  

**There are many moments when you are shaken to the core and the world knows nothing; when you cross mountains and the earth stays silent.**

---

# Daybreak, a lone shadow 

**"Ah, Denji. A country mouse or a city mouse: which would you choose?"**  

I have long known that loneliness is some kind of factory setting for humans.  
Sometimes looking at myself in the mirror, that person seems like a stranger.  
If studying, working, living are all just for some social rule, then where exactly am "I"?  

I do not want to live as a rigid footnote.  
Even if I get bloodied, even if I regret it, I want to make a sound of my own.  
So I built this website.  

It was my first attempt at building a castle of my own.  
In the cold wind of December I knew nothing about front-end or design, and sat staring at error messages.  
Others probably found it strange: "what is that person doing in front of the screen".  
But only I know that the moment the code ran, I was happier than I had been at any point.  

On Singles' Day I bought an electric guitar and downloaded arranging software.  
Theory is hard, my fingers hurt, and creating feels far more distant than I imagined.  
But that is fine.  
That road is calling my pack and my guitar. I have set off.
