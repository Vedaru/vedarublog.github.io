# How it started (the root of all evil?)

The first utaite song that got me into VOCALOID was Orangestar's Asu no Yozora Shoukaihan, but it was not the first one I ever heard.  

The first one I heard goes back to my third year of middle school, when I was very into the duo Yorushika. What I could not get enough of was the summer melancholy in n-buna's arrangements (surprise, I'm a n-buna fan too, hiahia, and suis's vocals are first-rate as well ~❤). Once I had nearly worn out Yorushika's songs, I went looking online to see whether n-buna had anything else. That is how I found his two Vocaloid albums.

# How I understand VOCALOID

The impact of n-buna's two Vocaloid albums and Orangestar's Shoukaihan was not limited to how they sound; for a long time they shaped how I understand utaite songs. (What follows is personal opinion; take it as entertainment 😛)  

Although the singer on n-buna's two albums is not a real person, the emotion in the songs, and the completeness of the songs and albums themselves, are in no way inferior to what n-buna wrote in the Yorushika era. That made me think: what exactly determines the emotion a song conveys?

# The "gods" who give electronic sound a soul

If VOCALOID software is an instrument, then the producer is the one who grants it divinity. After spending time in this scene, I found that every well-known producer has a very distinct personal scent.

For example, **n-buna**'s music always carries an inescapable sense of summer cicadas and a transparent melancholy, while **Orangestar** is more like a midsummer afternoon thunderstorm: oppressive with its extreme high register, yet leaving you washed clean afterwards.

*   **PinocchioP**: always singing the sharpest, most profound social observation over the most cheerful rhythm; that sense of "lucid absurdity" is addictive.
*   **MARETU**: that mechanical percussion and instantly recognisable twitching arrangement dissect the darkest emotions in a person completely.
*   **Kikuo**: genuine hallucinatory art, using Miku as the ritual instrument of some primal religion: gorgeous, eerie and pure.

You will notice that these creators do not treat Miku as a stand-in singer, but as an amplifier for their own soul. This distinctive structure of **creator over singer** is exactly what makes this scene so compelling.

# Not just music, but a culture of co-creation

I think VOCALOID has lasted twenty years partly because of its open nature. The birth of a great song often comes with a whole creative chain erupting at once.

<div class="post-side-by-side-two">
	<div class="post-text">
		<p>Often I fall for a song not just because of the melody. When I open the video and see the <b>illustration</b> sketching a grand world with simple colour blocks, and the designed <b>typography (the PV)</b> pulsing with the rhythm, that is when I truly understand the song. It is a composite art built from music, painting, narrative and technology.</p>
		<p>More romantic still, this scene has an extremely strong metabolism for derivative work. When a song gets popular, countless utaite will cover it and dancers will choreograph it. That point-to-surface co-creation stops VOCALOID from being merely software and makes it a living, evolving organism.</p>

# Closing: a colour that never faded

Many people predicted VOCALOID would decline, saying that with AI technology (SV, ACE and so on) the mechanical parameter-tweaking would lose its point. But I have always felt that **AI pursues "like a human", while VOCALOID pursues "beyond human".**

As I said earlier, that non-human quality, polished and tuned by hand countless times, is precisely the purest projection of human emotion. As long as someone is willing to stay up all night to get one portamento right, as long as someone can still be moved to tears by that crystalline electronic voice, the summer of VOCALOID will never end.

For me, since that afternoon in the third year of middle school when I first heard n-buna's "Hana to Mizuame, Saishuu Densha", some things have been fixed in place.

An eternal summer, an eternal Shoukaihan, and that leek-green figure who is forever sixteen and forever singing.
