# Absurdity

"Will AI replace humanity?"

Whenever I meet an interesting proposition like this, I want to share my view: "Yes, AI will eventually replace humanity. Let me tell you why: a human is, at most, a pile of cells arranged in a complicated form. All human emotion and consciousness are just emergence once complexity is high enough; on that point we are no different from AI. Human language, society and civilisation are all emergence once human complexity reaches a threshold, which is also the future of swarm AI. The whole human species is a brief instant in the vast universe, one tiny grain."

Then the teacher starts to frown and grows impatient. The teacher says: "Can AI imitate human feeling too? The richness of human emotion is something AI will never learn in a lifetime."

I smile. "Teacher, I have dissected the underlying logic of how human consciousness arises and analysed the underlying mechanism of AI intelligence, and found the two are very similar. I respect your view that AI cannot learn human emotion. Could you explain in detail why AI cannot imitate it?" The teacher starts to hem and haw, starts to go red. His volume seems, for some reason, to grow and grow, and then the teacher simply explodes.

Oh no, the teacher did not explode, because I did not say that. I only smiled and said, "Maybe." Making a teacher explode is something I would rather not cause.

"Lin Zexu suffered all his life, and yet never wavered in his resolve to defend the nation. If you lived in Lin Zexu's era, would you choose to be Lin Zexu? If you chose not to be Lin Zexu... ah, you must choose to be Lin Zexu, because you will be remembered in the history books." A modern history teacher said this to us in class one day.

In that rather telling "ah~" I tasted something absurd, and I wanted to laugh. And if I did not become Lin Zexu, what then? Would I be branded unpatriotic? Did Lin Zexu become Lin Zexu because he wanted to be remembered, and was his strength of will merely patriotic feeling and national spirit?

But from Lin Zexu's own point of view, he did not know the richly embellished, politically coloured "national spirit" of today, nor any notion of patriotism. He only knew he had a principle, and that following it brought his body and mind into a kind of self-consistency. And that framework of thought, tested by suffering, stayed solid. If other people's inner worlds of the time were outhouses that a gust of wind would knock down, Lin Zexu's mind was a fortress city, still holding firm with the enemy at the gates.

That solidity and self-consistency of thought has nothing to do with nation or nationality; it is the callus that grows on a person after deep introspection and endless self-dissection. It merely happens to overlap with national spirit and patriotism, and so some people feel the need to lecture others about loving the country and having national spirit? That is rather shallow. So history at this point is no longer being taught; it is being used.

# The scabbard of deconstruction is loneliness

The thinking above delighted me, the way a thief breaking into a jeweller's at midnight purely to smash things up and vent would be delighted. I tried to share that delight.

I told people: "A mother does not necessarily love her child just because she gave birth to it. So-called maternal love is only a hormonal reward mechanism in the brain; once that mechanism fails, the love is gone."

"Every emotional bond is a relationship of interest, because people derive mental pleasure from each other, and that is why the relationship is maintained."

"Culture and language are survival strategies for humans, no different in kind from the social and cooperative abilities of some herd animals; the human version is merely more complex."

But people did not seem pleased. First they wore an expression of puzzlement — that is them thinking (or not thinking so much as searching their own knowledge base) — then they grew impatient and wanted to argue back, but could find no grounds, so the older ones would shake their heads and curl a lip to show disapproval, trying to dissolve the awkwardness with the authority of an elder; or they would misread it as me showing off and, not wanting to be outdone, blurt out some cryptic "the Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao" or "three thousand worlds" style "wisdom". The younger ones would mumble, or simply decide I was an oddball suffering from a "disease of the century".

People are trapped in an iron house, and really the best thing for you, once you have broken through it, is to pretend to sleep among the sleepers.

But if you cannot accept that absurdity, then the only thing to do is sheathe the great sword of deconstruction in the scabbard of loneliness, and accept the world's exile.

# Standing on the ruins

When I came back to myself, I was at the oceanic pole of inaccessibility.

Under the swing of this great sword in my hand almost everything had become boring, cold and unrecognisable. But I could not stop, because the swinging of the blade was addictive; the sound of things cracking, shattering and exploding as they were cut thrilled me.

In the end, the only thing left to cut was myself.

Once there was a boy who was good at singing, and he sang of love, of youth, of all the beautiful things in the world. He did well at school and was the class monitor. The school arranged running exercises, and for each one every class had to march in neat formation and shout slogans loudly. As monitor he had to lead the shouting. However rowdy or perfunctory his classmates were, he never wavered or questioned; he kept shouting earnestly, even more loudly than before, but it was useless: the class stayed quiet and the teacher kept criticising the whole class.

He began to think: is it that I am not doing well enough? Is it the classmates? Is it the teacher? None of it seemed right. Is it the school? Is it... then one day he finally understood: although humans have high intelligence and strong consciousness, they are fundamentally still animals. And animals only want to live in the easiest way; since someone in the herd goes out to hunt, I need not trouble myself. The neat formation and the loud slogans once desired, and the "exercise" behind that fine name, were merely a convenient management trick invented by school leaders to please their own superiors.

It hurt, because a small piece of his former world suddenly collapsed, but more than that he felt a thrill: the relief of a question suddenly solved after long thought, and the appreciation of an elegant answer.

But the thrill did not last, because he found that all the long shouting had irreversibly damaged his vocal cords. He hurt again, and thought again — or rather, it was less thinking than a kind of virtual revenge: he took apart the intention behind his parents' comfort, and prised open the allocation of responsibility behind his teachers' encouragement. He would feel pain, and with the pain came a thrill. In his inner world he was a tyrant smashing everything.

The great sword passed through his body. He did not explode, did not break into fragments like the other things. But he became transparent, the world became transparent, everything became transparent, and at the same time bland.

He even thought that years later, writing this passage, he might himself give a small laugh: "what pseudo-philosophy wrapped in adolescent angst".

# The spider on the balcony

I was idling on the balcony, and a spider, all black with long thin legs, lay as motionless as I did. I began to talk with it, and before I noticed, a story about an absurd lone hero who destroys everything and finally returns to nothing had drifted away on the breeze.

After I noticed, I swatted the spider with a slipper. It knew too much.

But I felt an excitement I had never felt before. It was something that belonged only to me, entirely independent of that battered world. I knew every detail of it, and even if I took it fully apart I could reassemble it whole.

So the way to oppose the self-referential trap of deconstruction is to create meaning. The ultimate answer to absurdity is not to stop deconstructing, but to let deconstruction become the greediest and most faithful fuel for creation.

And so that boy, who could no longer sing, began to learn to compose the music of logic with code, to arrange matrices of meaning with words, to calibrate the frequency within with silence. He lost the vocal cords of the body, and tried to forge for himself a throat of thought.

Like a diesel engine: every moment it runs it is exploding, violently breaking down long-chain organic compounds into small-molecule exhaust. The only way to keep it running is an endless supply of fuel. But opposing deconstruction does not mean fully resolving it. Perhaps one day there will be no diesel left for this engine to breathe in, and it will breathe its own exhaust, accelerating madly until the cylinder block cracks, and the lubricating oil seeping into the cylinder accelerates it further, until past its limit it disintegrates entirely in one great roar.

Every exploding pain is the powerful pulse of the engine; every shudder is heavy breathing. I do not know when the diesel engine will run away, and perhaps it is happening right now.

This seemingly forced creation is a little helpless, as absurd as winning first place in running simply because you had to sprint to the canteen after every class.

But the only thing I can do now is create madly.
