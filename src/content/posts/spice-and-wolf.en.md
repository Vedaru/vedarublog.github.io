# Merchant Meets the Wise Wolf  

## Echoes of a pastoral idyll  

If I had to categorise anime, I would never file Spice and Wolf under "romance". What takes up more of the story is the rapport between Lawrence and Holo, a little stubborn but instantly in tune with each other. Holo is first Lawrence's travelling companion and only then his lover; their relationship begins as a contract to take her home, and somewhere along the road it turns into something deeper. The material looks well-worn, and precisely because it is well-worn, good writing and characterisation stand out all the more, and their kept-distance intimacy becomes something worth savouring.

<p><img src="/images/holo-and-lawrence.webp" alt="holo and lawrence" title="holo and lawrence"></p>

There are plenty of shows about abandoning your old job or life to start fresh in the countryside, but none of them, in my view, compare to Spice and Wolf, because of its grand world-building and continuous plot. This work is not merely a fantasy about a merchant and a wolf girl falling in love; it is a deeper parable: the wolf, or the spice?

---  

## Wolf? Spice?  

"The ground was covered in sixpences, yet he looked up and saw the moon."

But the moon smells wonderful, and so do all the sixpences on the ground. Choosing between them takes real nerve. Is there a way to have both? 😎 There is, brothers, there is. Lawrence shows us what it means to keep both the person and the profit.

First, Amati wants to pay off Holo's "enormous debt" so he can marry her. Lawrence signs a contract with Amati for deferred delivery of pyrite; Lawrence and Holo coordinate, dumping huge quantities of pyrite and crashing the pyrite stock, and Amati goes bankrupt 😀. Let us salute the legendary merchant Amati (man!). But look closer: without Holo's final pyrite dump, it is hard to say whether Amati might have succeeded. The bond between Holo and Lawrence is far more than fellow travellers. Before Lawrence bought his large position, Holo had already, in perfect coordination, bought pyrite herself (with Amati's money, no less 🤡). Holo even tucked Diana's feather into her headscarf, but old Lawrence the blockhead didn't catch the meaning; the upshot is that Amati became a prop in Lawrence and Holo's little game.

To the world, Holo and Lawrence are just business partners, their relationship held together by material interest and money. But when Lawrence is set up by the trading houses, left with a cartload of armour he cannot sell and up to his ears in debt, how many of those "brothers" who called him brother were willing to lend him money to get through? Those people looked down on Lawrence merely because of the Holo standing beside him, while Holo, even when she cannot help, stays with him late into the night, and when he grows impatient she only blames herself. That is not a relationship a contract can measure. Watching that part was painful. I understand Lawrence's anxiety over his huge debt, but money is not the most important thing in a life; the sincere bond between people is. Fortunately he returns to the inn, sees Holo blaming herself, and apologises first, and in the end the old wolf comes up with a scheme to smuggle gold, which earns the money to clear the debt. If you have the wolf, why worry about the spice?

Yet in reality, how many of those who have signed a "marriage" contract end up closer than those who never signed one?

## Twilight of the gods and the turning of the age

One of the most captivating things about Spice and Wolf is the world it builds, standing on the eve of something: reason (commerce and contract) is replacing feeling (faith and miracles), and humans are replacing gods.

Holo, the wise wolf of Yoitsu, is essentially an old god the age has forgotten. People once revered her and prayed for a good harvest; later they grew tired of her and called her capricious; later still, with fertiliser and irrigation, they dismissed her as an obsolete legend. In the eyes of Lawrence the merchant, the world is numbers on an abacus; in Holo's eyes, it is a thousand years of loneliness.

This **collision between divinity and humanity** plays out most clearly in the contest between the Church's power and commercial interest. When the Church frames heretics to protect its authority, and lords start wars for wealth, the so-called holy light is no nobler than a merchant's coin. It is precisely amid all that scheming, smelling of money, that the wolf-eared Holo, who loves to drink and occasionally sulks yet sees straight through people, seems the most human of all.

## The end of the journey, or the start of a life?

Later in the novels (and in the follow-up Wolf and Parchment), the centre of gravity shifts from "how to make money" to "how to live together". That is what I think makes this work great: it does not only discuss the hardship of starting out, it confronts the eternal, cruel proposition of **the long-lived and the short-lived**.

Holo says she is afraid of loneliness. For someone with so long a lifespan, Lawrence's life is the blink of an eye. If parting is inevitable, does meeting still mean anything? The original anime stops abruptly at season two, leaving only longing and the dream of Yoitsu; the novels give the warmest possible answer: **Nyohhira.**

When Lawrence gives up his dream of becoming a great merchant and settles in the hot-spring town, he has already completed the shift from "a man earning sixpences" to "a man guarding the moon". They no longer rush from deal to deal; they face groceries and the occasional tantrum of the "wolf proprietress". That ordinariness is the finest coronation for the thrilling journey before it.

---

## Rambling: the remake and nostalgia

As someone who watched the original anime and read every volume of the novels, seeing the 2024 remake left me with mixed feelings.

The original (2008/2009), with its slightly cold filter full of medieval European oil-painting texture, and the near-irreplaceable voices of Ami Koshimizu and Jun Fukuyama, has become a footnote to an era. The new version is more polished and truer to Jyuu Ayakura's character designs, but sometimes I still miss that particular texture in the old one, as if the air carried the smell of wheat and horse dung.

Still, just as Holo always finds something new in an old apple, as long as that signature bagpipe starts up, as long as Lawrence is clutching his purse in resignation while Holo swishes her tail in triumph beside him, I know that journey of wolf and spice will never really end.

---

## Closing

If most anime lets you escape reality, Spice and Wolf makes you fall in love with living. It tells us that money matters, that it is the ground under our feet; but the person willing to earn that coin alongside you, to hold an umbrella in the rain and share a drink on a snowy night, is our only support in a cold world.

After all, **"A merchant always chases profit, and my greatest profit is you."**

---
*Note: if you haven't read the novels, I strongly recommend them. It is not just a light novel but a romantic course in medieval commercial sociology.*
