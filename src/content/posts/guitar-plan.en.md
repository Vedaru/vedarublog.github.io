# Electric guitar and VOCALOID 

Why would I connect two things that have nothing to do with each other?  

Well, the first thing I got into was [VOCALOID](/en/posts/vocaloid), but after transcribing songs for a while the idea of writing my own took root, so I vaguely downloaded Cubase too. Arranging in Cubase got me nowhere for a while, so I thought I should learn some music theory; honestly it is fairly abstract, and after a while I only half understood it.  

They say practice teaches truth, so I thought: if I learn an instrument first and practise through playing, theory would become far more intuitive. So I made this plan.  

The plan I started with is not perfect, and I will keep updating it in later posts.  
I hope what I record can be useful to others with the same interests 😊!

---

# Teaching myself electric guitar

Deciding to self-teach electric guitar was not a whim; I thought it through carefully (my coursework is heavy, so there is not much spare time).  

I weighed up both the theory-and-practice side and the budget, but there are many ways to learn, and my first draft still has gaps.  

So treat this plan as reference only, and as a record of my state of mind while preparing to self-teach.

---

## Gear

### The guitar

I did not know much about guitar brands or models. All I could think of was the Telecaster solos in Nakanaka's early utaite songs, so I narrowed my search to Telecasters. Online I then learned about Fender, Gibson, Ibanez and others, and in the end I chose a Fender Squier Sonic Tele in buttercream yellow.


<p><img src="/images/squier-sonic.webp" alt="Squier Sonic buttercream" title="Squier Sonic buttercream"></p>

That buttercream yellow is genuinely lovely; you can almost feel a warm, heavy, silky touch.  

Reasons for choosing it:  
First, the Squier is excellent value. I listened to demos on YouTube and Bilibili and liked the tone, and it costs barely over a thousand.  
Second, I am not aiming high with this guitar; it only needs to meet basic practice needs, and if I later want to try DIY, modding this one will not break my heart.

### Amp

I asked a classmate who has played electric guitar for years, and they recommended the jambuddy.


<p><img src="/images/jambuddy.webp" alt="jambuddy amp" title="jambuddy amp"></p>

Honestly the amp is very good, but playing it in the dorm would annoy my roommates, and plugging headphones into the amp defeats the point of buying one. Also I am too poor for effects pedals, and expanding later would not be kind to my wallet.  

So the solution I settled on was to buy an audio interface, use Cubase for virtual tones and effects, and monitor with headphones. Kind to my wallet and kind to my roommates (  

Though wearing headphones all the time is uncomfortable, so I probably still need an amp; as a beginner, headphone monitoring is just about enough.

### Audio interface

After buying the guitar my wallet was basically empty, leaving almost nothing for an interface, so I initially set the budget at an absurd 400 (I know it is ridiculous, but poverty makes you bold). Looking at the market, I found a guitar-specific USB interface called jogg for just over 300, and almost bought it, but on reading the specs there was no solid data on its noise floor, so out of caution I dropped the jogg.  

At that price there really was nothing else decent, so I changed tactics: buy a guitar headphone amp first so I could hear the guitar and practise, and save for an interface later. Saving meant squeezing the budget again, and I ended up with the Valeton Rushhead Max.


<p><img src="/images/valeton-rushhead-max.webp" alt="Valeton Rushhead Max" title="Valeton Rushhead Max"></p>

When it arrived I plugged in and tried it, and my immediate impression was that the noise floor was really high, and it grew as I turned the guitar's volume knob up; switching from clean to distortion made the noise explode. After a few days I could not stand it and started looking for a proper interface. I looked for recommendations on social media and found mostly the Focusrite 2i2 4th gen and the Steinberg UR22, but those are a bit pricey and a bit old, so on YouTube I settled on the Arturia MiniFuse 2 and the Steinberg UR22MK3.  

<p><img src="/images/UR22MK3.webp" alt="UR22MK3" title="UR22MK3"></p>

I had also been drawn to another interface called the Steinberg IXO22, whose hardware is identical to the UR22MK3 and cheaper. Later I found out that the UR22MK3 is simply the model name the IXO22 took after being folded into YAMAHA's product line. In the end I still chose the more expensive UR22MK3, because YAMAHA as Steinberg's parent company will surely have optimised it more specifically (my own opinion), so the UR22MK3's drivers should be better (otherwise there would be no reason for it to cost more).

<p><img src="/images/ixo22.webp" alt="ixo22" title="ixo22"></p>

### Software

For a DAW I started with FL Studio, but after a while it felt like FL suits electronic music better; writing rock or jazz or that kind of popular music always felt a bit off.  
Also FL's theme colours are honestly a bit ugly and the workflow is somewhat counter-intuitive. In the end I used Cubase 14 Pro (which of course I got from a website 😀).  

Cubase feels clear and very professional, with extremely sensible keybindings, though the wall of buttons can be off-putting at first.
